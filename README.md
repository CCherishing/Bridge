# Bridge

Bridge is my project for helping people find opportunities, learn in public, and think out loud.

## My purpose

I am building Bridge for disadvantaged high school students, especially students from first-generation and low-income backgrounds who may not know which opportunities exist or how to reach them. I want to collect and explain useful resources such as scholarships, programs, internships, mentorship, financial aid, and practical next steps.

I know that information alone does not solve every barrier. My goal is to make opportunity information easier to discover, easier to understand, and less intimidating to act on.

## What I want to make

Bridge will grow into three connected parts:

- **The Opportunity Map:** carefully organized resources for students and families.
- **Learning in Public:** notes about my progress learning AI, coding, Java, web development, and related subjects.
- **The Notebook:** an old-style personal blog for philosophy, technology, observations, and unfinished thoughts.

I want the site to feel useful and human rather than like a polished corporate product. Some pages will be practical guides, while others will simply document what I am learning and thinking.

## How I plan to build it

I am deliberately starting with the fundamentals instead of immediately hiding them behind a framework.

My initial stack will be:

1. **HTML** for structure and accessible, semantic pages.
2. **CSS** for layout, typography, responsive design, and the visual identity.
3. **Vanilla JavaScript** for small interactive features.
4. **Java** as the main programming language I am learning.
5. **Spring Boot** later, when the project needs a backend, APIs, accounts, search, or user-submitted resources.
6. **SQLite** at first for simple data storage, with PostgreSQL as a possible later step.

I expect to begin with a mostly static website. As I understand the fundamentals better, I will build a small Java backend and connect it to the frontend. The important parts of the application should remain understandable to me, even as the project becomes more capable.

## How I will use AI

I will use AI as a tutor, reviewer, and pair-programming assistant—not as a replacement for learning.

I want to write the important code myself, especially the parts that teach me how the system works. I will use AI to:

- explain unfamiliar concepts in plain language;
- help me compare possible approaches;
- generate small exercises and boilerplate after I understand the purpose;
- review code that I wrote;
- help me find bugs and think through edge cases;
- suggest tests and documentation;
- help organize research and identify gaps in the resource directory;
- show me examples that I can study, rewrite, and improve.

When AI generates code, I will read it, run it, question it, and rewrite it when necessary. I do not want to copy code that I cannot explain. I also want to verify information before publishing it, especially when it concerns scholarships, deadlines, eligibility, or other opportunities that could affect students.

## My learning principles

- I will prefer understanding over speed.
- I will build small features before combining them into larger systems.
- I will keep notes about mistakes, not only successes.
- I will learn the boilerplate by writing it repeatedly.
- I will use frameworks after learning the underlying web concepts they automate.
- I will treat accessibility, clarity, privacy, and accuracy as core features.
- I will ask for help from AI without outsourcing my judgment.

## Current goal

My first goal is not to build a perfect platform. It is to create a small, honest website that helps someone find a useful opportunity while giving me a place to practice HTML, CSS, Java, writing, research, and responsible use of AI.
